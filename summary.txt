World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with no ceasefire, but agreement on a record prisoner exchange, 1,000 to 1,500 a side, as US envoys headed to Moscow (Reuters, NPR). Kyiv rejected a reported US proposal to redraw Donbas borders so Russia could claim the whole region (The Telegraph). Russia struck six power substations in Ukraine, and a market attack in the Odesa region killed three (Reuters). NATO scrambled jets after at least four drones strayed into Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia (Reuters). Nvidia's board ousted chief executive Jensen Huang over two reported affairs, one with a woman US officials suspect of spying for China; finance chief Colette Kress was named interim CEO and shares fell over 8% after hours (Reuters, Bloomberg). Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's US Senate seat, the AP called, beating Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 4,700 votes to become the state's first Black senator since Reconstruction, lifting Democrats to 55 seats (AP). At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned slow climate action had put the world "on a path to destruction" (Reuters). The US charged three Iran-backed operatives over about 20 plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford, and Treasury chief Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Saudi defences intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Riyadh, Mecca and other regions (Reuters). Brent crude rose 1.4% to $92.76 a barrel as Iran denied talks with Washington and a Saudi tanker was hit in the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters). Wall Street rebounded, the S&P 500 up 1.3%, the Nasdaq 1.6% and the Dow 0.5%, as CoreWeave jumped almost 25% (Reuters). The House voted 211-204 to send eight articles impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, blocking the effort (AP). British police arrested a US Army officer with a NATO unit on suspicion of spying for Russia (AP).
